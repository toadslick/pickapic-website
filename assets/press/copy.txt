ABOUT PICK-A-PIC
‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾

Pick-a-Pic is an AAC (augmentative and alternative communication) app for iPad focused on ease-of-use for early learners and their caregivers. It allows caregivers to quickly turn their images into buttons that speak when tapped.

Pick-a-Pic was created as a springboard for teaching functional communication to children with autism. Functional communication is an evidence-based intervention for replacing challenging behaviors with basic social interaction. The basics of functional communication, such as making requests, are where Pick-a-Pic’s simplicity shines.

Pick-a-Pic seeks to address the common frustrations that learners and their caregivers experience with more complex AAC apps:

- AAC apps may require many steps to create a custom button — a barrier to creating buttons at a moment when they are urgently needed.
- The steep learning curve of AAC apps can dissuade learners and caregivers from using them.
- Large grids of images can be overwhelming for learners with limited visual discrimination.
- Learners can have difficulty associating generic illustrations with the specific thing they want to request. For example, they may not equate an icon of a teddy bear with their favorite stuffed animal.

Pick-a-Pic addresses these frustrations with a user interface that is intuitive and familiar to iPad users and that is designed to minimize the number of taps required to create a custom button in a Button Book.

Each Pick-a-Pic file is organized into a Picture Library and a collection of Button Books. Pictures are imported into the Picture Library and then added to pages of Button Books, where they become speech-generating buttons. Pictures can be imported from a variety of sources: the camera, photo library, files, and anywhere that allows copy-and-paste, such as web browsers. Pictures can also be created from emoji or text, and Pick-a-Pic also includes a small (but growing) gallery of free images.

Caregivers can customize the voice of each Button Book and have the option to lock Button Books with either Face ID or a 4-digit passcode. When combined with iPad’s Guided Access feature, this allows caregivers to turn their iPad into a dedicated AAC device.

Pick-a-Pic has no registration, no login, and no subscription. Users can begin using the app right away for free, and pay only a small, one-time charge to unlock the full app across all of the devices in their Family Sharing plan.

KEY FEATURES
‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾

Turn your images into buttons that speak when tapped.
  Import images from your device's camera, photo library, files, or any app that supports copy-and-paste, such as your web browser, or create images from emoji or text.

Organize your buttons into Button Books.
  Buttons automatically arrange themselves neatly on a page. Drag to rearrange buttons or to reorder the pages of a Button Book.
  Customize the voice of each Button Book. Lock Button Books with either Face ID or a 4-digit passcode.

Made for collaboration between caregivers and professionals.
  Share and sync Pick-a-Pic files through iCloud, Google Drive, or Dropbox.

Pick-a-Pic fully respects your privacy.
  No registration or login. No analytics or tracking.

ABOUT THE DEVELOPER
‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾

Pocket Pigeon is a project of James Childress and Selina Field.

James is a software developer and Selina is a BCBA (board-certified behavior analyst) with a specialty in verbal behavior training for autistic children.

Pick-a-Pic was borne from Selina's years of experience as a BCBA, in which she regularly witnessed caregivers and coworkers struggle to customize their AAC apps.
